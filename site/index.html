See small drones in RGB and thermal video.

Detection, tracking and threat assessment of small drones from a visible camera and a thermal camera, with YOLO11 models trained on the real Anti-UAV300 benchmark.

The tracker keeps the detector's hits and bridges the frames it misses, so the tracked system finds the drone in more frames than the detector alone (75.7% RGB and 62.0% infrared). On a laptop GPU the pipeline processed a 1080p test video at about 37 frames per second, with detection taking about 10 ms per frame.

Pipeline

How it works

DetectionYOLO11s per camera, fine-tuned on Anti-UAV300. A training-free fallback for clean sky uses morphological saliency with an adaptive threshold.
FusionThermal boxes are mapped into the RGB image through a calibrated homography and paired. Either camera can drop out and tracking continues on the other.
TrackingA constant-velocity Kalman filter per target, with gates for jerky camera motion, re-acquisition after jumps and coasting through dropouts.
ClassificationExplainable log-odds over about 45 frames: detector label and confidence, cross-sensor consistency, wing-beat, turn rate and hover.
ThreatBearing, elevation, rough range and closing speed, then NONE, LOW, MEDIUM or HIGH levels with hysteresis so alerts do not flicker.

The operator view

The repository also contains a small web dashboard (sign-in, a live view with drone counts, a sky-dome plot and an alert feed). It needs a long-running detector and a GPU for good speed, so it runs locally or on a GPU host rather than on this page.

Honest limits

What it does not do yet

Research use only. This is a prototype, not an operational system.
  • Unfamiliar footage. Training data is drone-only sky footage from specific sites. Expect misses and false alarms on scenes it has not seen, such as faces, indoor objects or heavy clutter, until it is fine-tuned on local footage.
  • Range is approximate. It is estimated from the apparent size with an assumed drone span. True range needs radar, RF or a calibrated stereo pair.
  • Two-camera fusion. On Anti-UAV the RGB and infrared videos are not pixel-aligned, so fused mode scores below single-camera mode. Per-camera operation is the recommended setup.
  • Clutter and the simple detector. The training-free detector flags any high-contrast spot. Near buildings it produced about 30 false drones per clip in our tests, so the dashboard uses the trained model only.
